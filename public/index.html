Signed PDFs

Adobe Acrobat may say my signature's identity is unknown. That's expected: my certificate comes from my own certificate authority rather than a paid one. To trust it once for every document I sign:

  1. Click my signature, then Signature Properties → Show Signer's Certificate.
  2. Select the top certificate and check its SHA-1 matches DE:03:4F:80:E6:69:4B:54:42:50:A6:1B:E0:B3:BA:9C:FE:A3:1D:23.
  3. On the Trust tab, choose Add to Trusted Certificates and tick Use this certificate as a trusted root.

Email and files

Use my PGP key to send me something encrypted or to check something I've signed. Most PGP tools can fetch it from my email address:

gpg --locate-keys ben@bensoyka.com

Code and servers

My Git commits are signed with my PGP key, so GitHub marks them Verified. To give me SSH access, add my SSH key:

How to trust this page

Everything here comes from keys.txt, which is signed with my main PGP key. A website can be tampered with, so for anything important, get my key from somewhere else too (keys.openpgp.org, GitHub, or me in person) and check:

curl -s https://keys.bensoyka.com/keys.txt | gpg --verify

The signature should come from BC48 76BD C8AB 5AB8 C9CB 8165 9497 E305 83D6 8D89.

My domains and accounts are linked to this key on Keyoxide, which checks each link independently.
